# Suggestions

Ideas noticed during work, not yet asked for. Strike or promote as you see fit.

- **Remaining tight spots between the populated board and the tray after the ledge reliefs.** Measured 2026-10-11 with FreeCAD on LibrePCB's populated STEP export against the trays `scripts/export_tray.py` builds, board resting on the standoffs (probe in `tmp/check_fit.py`, not committed). The ledge is now relieved beside the nano and beside every cavity-side socket that came within 0.5 mm of it (`relief_clearance`), so what is left: the west socket tabs (S100 to S103) end 0.17 mm from the wall inside their notches (a shallow recess into the wall there, or `gap=0.5`, would fix it); socket S150 is 0.50 mm from the standoff at (94.77, 60.40), S141 0.67 mm from the one at (76.04, 43.26), J1 0.76 mm from the one at (114.14, 19.77); the power switch body sits in its wall pocket 0.5 mm from the pocket's ends, and 0.89 mm from the pocket's back by the package model (0.73 mm by the 2026-10-12 measurement on the assembled print, which has the body 0.07 mm past the board edge where the model has it 0.09 mm inside). Measuring the printed cavity between the ledge faces (design 133.42 mm at the board's full width) would size the swelling to design against.
- **The plate STL is not a closed mesh.** `scripts/export_plate.py` meshes the plate as a compound of touching cell solids (plus the tab and now the corner slab), so the STL carries coincident internal walls at every cell boundary: FreeCAD reports it non-manifold with hundreds of self-intersections, for the July 2026 export and the current one alike (the tray and PCB replica STLs are clean). Slicers have evidently coped so far. Fusing the solids once (`multiFuse` plus `removeSplitter`, which `drill_holes` already does for its connectivity check) before `export_stl` would give a single closed shell. Noticed 2026-10-08 while verifying the corner extension; left alone because it changes the shipped mesh of a part that already prints.
- **The printed plate's holes drift about 1 mm toward the nice!nano side over the plate's width; the cause is not found.** Checked 2026-10-09: `boards/board_1.1/board.lp` has every switch pitch at exactly 19.05 mm, the committed Excellon files put the 3.4 mm switch drills and the 2.4 mm mounting drills at the same positions, the kbdkid3 plate model's cutouts match the switch grid to 0.0005 mm, the exported plate STL has every cutout, screw hole and the reset hole at its design x within 0.08 mm, and the tray STL's standoff bores sit at the mounting drills to 0.001 mm. Both parts were printed unscaled. A 1 mm drift at 125 mm is 0.8 %, the size of a print scale or shrinkage error, but a plate shrunk that much should sit loose in the tray, which it does not. `pattern_shift` in `scripts/export_plate.py` corrects it empirically: a 0.5 mm shift came out offset about as far the other way (printed 2026-10-10; a linear 1 mm drift would have left both ends 0.5 mm off in opposite directions instead), so the default is 0.25 mm now. To find the cause, measure: the printed plate's width (design 135.05 mm for the plates printed so far, 134.85 mm since the side shave of 2026-10-10) and the span from the left edge of the bottom-left cutout to the right edge of the thumb cutout (design 128.4 mm), the PCB's two bottom mounting holes centre to centre (design 95.57 mm), and the printed tray's outer width (design 137.02 mm).
- **The right tray's nano ledge reliefs are too shallow for the 4.3 mm sockets planned for the right half.** `scripts/export_tray.py` takes the nano's depth from its package model (`MCU_BODY_RECTANGLES_MM`, nano PCB 2.75 to 4.15 mm below the board), which matches neither half: the left nano sits flush on 2.56 mm Mill-Max sockets, the right one will sit on 4.3 mm ones (user, 2026-10-12). On the right, the nano's PCB would then span 2.30 to 3.70 mm above the tray floor, while the reliefs beside it stop at 3.35 mm, so its north edge (0.03 mm from the ledge) and east edge (0.27 mm) would bind like the left half's first print did. A per-half socket height feeding the nano's reach would fix it; held back because the user wants the right half's connector measured once soldered.
- **The model does not reproduce the measured USB-C connector height on the left half.** The user's pull-ins put the receptacle shell's center about 2.60 mm below the board's underside; chaining the board underside at 8.0 mm above the floor (standoff height), the nano flush on 2.56 mm sockets and the package model's receptacle centered 0.60 mm below the nano's face gives 3.16 mm, 0.56 mm lower. Either the M2 heat-set inserts stand proud of the standoffs and lift the board, or the nice!nano model's receptacle offset differs from the real part. Measuring the board underside's height above the tray floor, or the shell's far side from the nano face resting on the sockets (2.18 mm in the model), tells which, and the right half's window and reliefs should be derived from the corrected chain.
